## What Actually Counts as a Welldweller Location

A welldweller location is any spot where the creature can anchor itself and cycle into view. In most builds, that means a vertical shaft, a covered well opening, a flooded hollow, or a collapsed cistern connected to an underground water table. The common thread is enclosure plus water — welldwellers rarely appear in open ground.

Players often confuse "a place it can be" with "a place it reliably is." Many shafts qualify structurally but only produce a welldweller under specific conditions, such as after a certain story beat, during a particular weather cycle, or once a nearby area has been cleared.

| Location Type | Typical Access | Reported Reliability | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Village well | Open, walk-up | High | Most consistent early sightings |
| Flooded cistern | Requires light source | Medium | Often tied to a puzzle state |
| Collapsed shaft | Drop-down, one-way | Medium | Bring a way back up |

Community reports consistently note that the first type on that list is where most players get their initial encounter, which makes village wells the smartest place to start any route.

## Verifying a Spawn Without Wasting a Trip

Verification is where most hunters lose time. A single pass through a location proves very little, because welldwellers are frequently conditional. Follow a fixed routine so your results mean something.

| Step | Action | What You're Looking For |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear nearby enemies | Removes a common blocking condition |
| 2 | Check the water surface | Ripples, disturbed silt, or movement |
| 3 | Wait one full cycle | Confirms whether timing matters |
| 4 | Leave and return | Rules out a one-time trigger |
| 5 | Log the result | Builds your personal reliability data |

Steps three and four matter most. A site that produces nothing on a first visit but works on a return trip is almost certainly condition-based, not broken. Note the conditions — time of day, weather, story progress — and you'll usually crack the pattern within a few attempts.

## Practical Tips From the Community

- **Route by region, not by distance.** Batching sites in one area cuts travel time dramatically.
- **Always carry a light source.** A large share of reported sites sit in unlit spaces.
- **Revisit before you give up.** A no-show on the first pass means very little.
- **Clear first, hunt second.** Hostile populations appear to block some triggers.
- **Note the conditions, not just the result.** Weather and story progress frequently matter.
- **Don't trust a single source.** One report is a rumor; three matching reports is a pattern.

One recurring theme in community reports is that players who record conditions find their target far faster than players who simply revisit blindly. Data beats persistence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many welldweller all locations are there in total?**
There's no fixed number that stays accurate across updates. The count changes as areas are added or adjusted, which is exactly why a category-based checklist holds up better than a hard list of pins.

**Do welldweller all locations respawn after you find one?**
In most cases yes, but not on a simple timer. Many sites appear to depend on conditions rather than a cooldown, so leaving the area and returning after a state change is usually more effective than waiting in place.

**Why does a confirmed site stop working for me?**
The most common causes reported by players are unresolved nearby enemies, a missed weather or time window, or incomplete progress in the surrounding area. Work through those three before assuming the site is bugged.

**Is it worth using a map mod or third-party tracker?**
It can help with routing, but treat the data as a hint rather than a guarantee. Verify anything you rely on, and keep your own log as the source of truth — that's the only list you know is current.
